# MIT Maker Portfolio Strategy

This document is a presentation plan, not an admissions promise. RPA-1 should remain worth building even if it is never submitted anywhere.

## Current official guidance to design around

MIT describes Maker Portfolio projects as substantial, original, and technically creative, evaluated with engineering critical-thinking standards. The current guidance permits up to 25 media attachments, asks for abundant build-process evidence and clear explanations, accepts work in progress when completion is represented honestly, limits total video to two minutes, requires video for moving mechanisms, and requires code projects to include the codebase plus a working demonstration.

Sources:

- [MIT Admissions — Creative portfolios](https://mitadmissions.org/apply/firstyear/portfolios-additional-material/)
- [MIT Admissions — Extracurricular activities](https://mitadmissions.org/apply/prepare/activities/)

Recheck the official requirements during the actual application cycle because limits and submission systems can change.

## Strong project thesis

> I investigated whether a low-cost robot with fivefold radial symmetry could reuse the same compliant limb architecture for stable locomotion and expressive manipulation. I built increasingly capable prototypes, measured competing artificial-muscle technologies, documented unsafe or impractical ideas, and revised the design from evidence.

This thesis is stronger than “I tried to build a movie character” because it exposes an original engineering question while retaining the personal motivation and visual goal.

## Evidence to collect continuously

1. Early sketches with dated assumptions
2. Reference-confidence and copyright decisions
3. First breadboard and first software output
4. Failed actuator specimens and what each failure changed
5. Raw force, response, hysteresis, current, temperature, and sound data
6. CAD evolution with mass and support-polygon overlays
7. Safety mechanisms and intentional fault injection
8. A mentor review showing which work was yours and which advice you received
9. Git history connecting requirements, code, tests, and revisions
10. A working scale robot performing motion that cannot be understood from still images alone

## Candidate 25-item media storyboard

Do not fill every slot unless each adds evidence.

1. One-sentence problem and final hero image
2. Movie/book/assumption evidence taxonomy
3. First system block diagram
4. Arduino communicator breadboard
5. Musical-language token diagram
6. Encoder/decoder test result
7. Common actuator test rig
8. Electric tendon specimen
9. Pneumatic specimen behind guard
10. Manual syringe hydraulic specimen
11. Comparative force-displacement plot
12. Hysteresis plot and interpretation
13. Selected actuator decision matrix
14. First tendon joint failure
15. Improved series-elastic joint
16. Three-DOF limb workspace
17. Three-finger compliant gripper
18. Scale body mass/COM measurement
19. Five-contact support polygon
20. First successful step
21. Turn and mode-transition state machine
22. Explanation-theatre prop sequence
23. E-stop and communications-loss demonstration
24. BOM and $100/month cost history
25. Final status: completed, incomplete, and next research question

## Two-minute motion reel

- 0:00–0:12 — research question and fivefold constraint
- 0:12–0:32 — three actuator specimens on the same rig
- 0:32–0:48 — data plot and design revision
- 0:48–1:08 — three-DOF tendon limb and compliant claw
- 1:08–1:32 — scale pentapod walking and turning with support polygon
- 1:32–1:48 — three-leg/two-arm transition and prop explanation
- 1:48–2:00 — fault stop, current status, and next question

Use text overlays or concise voiceover. Do not spend the limited runtime on cinematic introductions.

## Authorship record

For every subsystem, record:

- what you designed and built;
- libraries or open designs used and their licenses;
- fabrication performed by a makerspace or mentor;
- advice received;
- measurements and analysis you personally performed;
- changes you made after failure.

Collaboration is valuable. Ambiguous authorship is not.

## Application discipline

- Do not postpone academics, sleep, or safety to manufacture an “impressive” result.
- Do not claim the final robot exists when only a render or subsystem exists.
- Do not hide failures; show the consequential ones concisely.
- Do not choose hydraulics, AI, LiDAR, or expensive parts merely for prestige.
- Prefer one well-measured original mechanism over ten shallow features.
