# Sophomore-Year Portfolio Roadmap

**Planning window:** October 2026–May 2028
**Budget ceiling:** $100/month and $2,000 cumulative
**Assumption:** sophomore year ends in May 2028; update this file when the academic calendar is confirmed.

The objective is a sequence of defensible results, not a rushed full-scale walker. Each milestone must produce a working demonstration, measurements, source files, failures, and a decision.

## Milestone 0 — Repository and measurement discipline

**Due:** November 2026
**Incremental budget:** $0–75
**Result:** repository, requirements, risk register, communicator, and CSP language test.

Acceptance:

- README accurately identifies completed versus planned work.
- Arduino communicator plays at least four defined musical tokens.
- Physical translation button works for 100 presses with no more than one missed or duplicate toggle.
- Software round trip encodes and decodes at least 100 generated valid messages with ≥99% exact semantic equality.
- First circuit, source code, BOM, wiring, test log, and short video are published.

## Milestone 1 — Artificial-muscle comparison

**Due:** February 2027
**Incremental budget:** $175; cumulative target $250
**Result:** common test joint operated by three low-energy specimens:

1. electric motor/servo with tendon and series spring;
2. rated or conservatively constructed low-pressure pneumatic specimen;
3. manual water-filled syringe hydraulic specimen.

Acceptance:

- same joint, lever arm, angle range, load points, and data sheet for every specimen;
- force/travel, hysteresis, repeatability, response, mass, cost, noise, and failure observations recorded;
- pressure specimens guarded with minimal stored volume and no homemade tank;
- selected architecture justified by data rather than appearance.

Portfolio video: a split-screen comparison with plots and one honest failed trial.

## Milestone 2 — One role-fluid limb

**Due:** May 2027
**Incremental budget:** $300; cumulative target $550
**Result:** guarded three-DOF tendon-driven limb and compliant three-finger hand/foot.

Acceptance:

- local joint feedback and current measurement;
- repeatability ≤3° at released load;
- defined endpoint path with ≤20 mm RMS error at the selected scale;
- 500 low-speed cycles without damaged tendon or loose structure;
- E-stop, limit, stale-command, encoder-loss, overcurrent, and simulated tendon-slack tests pass;
- 50 g prop pickup and broad-pad load transfer both demonstrated.

Portfolio video: gesture, grip, place, foot-pad conversion, and two fault tests.

## Milestone 3 — 1:3 pentapod assembly

**Due:** September 2027
**Incremental budget:** $450; cumulative target $1,000
**Result:** complete lightweight five-limb platform, initially tethered.

Acceptance:

- 15 calibrated limb axes;
- five contact sensors and IMU reporting coherently;
- safe startup requires deliberate enable;
- no controller brownout during a 30-minute representative duty test;
- measured mass, center of mass, current envelope, and support polygons documented;
- each limb can be assigned the same software role interface.

Portfolio video: symmetric limb calibration and body pose control with telemetry overlay.

## Milestone 4 — Walking, turning, and arm transition

**Due:** December 2027
**Incremental budget:** $250; cumulative target $1,250
**Result:** slow five-limb wave gait and stationary three-leg/two-arm behavior.

Acceptance:

- one swing foot maximum during the first gait;
- 2 m forward course and 90° turn;
- stop after heartbeat loss or invalid contact;
- 20 consecutive five-foot → shifted tripod → one arm → two arms → five-foot transitions without fall or tether catch;
- support-polygon and estimated center-of-mass margin logged during every transition;
- no operation near bystanders, pets, or stairs.

Portfolio video: gait state and support polygon rendered alongside the physical robot.

## Milestone 5 — Musical assistant and explanation theatre

**Due:** March 2028
**Incremental budget:** $250; cumulative target $1,500
**Result:** deterministic musical language, translation controls, assistant, and prop choreography.

Acceptance:

- original musical sounds always remain audible when translation is enabled;
- physical mode button remains authoritative;
- voice and clap triggers cannot enable motion;
- six gestures are identified by at least 8 of 10 viewers;
- five standardized ≤50 g props are selected, sequenced, and returned in 19 of 20 supervised trials;
- AI-proposed movement is schema-validated and locally bounded;
- memory is opt-in, inspectable, and deletable.

Portfolio video: Rocky uses tokens and gestures to explain a simple engineering idea.

## Milestone 6 — Portfolio Release 1.0

**Due:** May 2028
**Incremental budget:** $250; cumulative target $1,750
**Contingency remaining:** $250
**Result:** reproducible technical release and lightweight full-scale stationary interaction form/module.

Acceptance:

- versioned code, CAD, schematics, BOM, requirements, risks, raw test data, and test reports;
- full-scale structure remains stationary or externally supported;
- all claims link to a test or are clearly labeled assumptions;
- build-process photographs include early failures and revisions;
- concise motion reel is no longer than two minutes;
- independent mentor reviews the safety boundaries and authorship record;
- a new builder can reproduce at least the communicator and one-joint experiment from the repository.

## What makes this a strong application project

- A distinctive, technically coherent research question
- Electrical, mechanical, controls, software, audio, AI, and safety integration
- Original fivefold locomotion/interaction problem rather than a copied hexapod kit
- Quantitative trade studies and visible revisions
- Real failures and responsible scope reduction
- Reproducible open engineering work
- A useful, expressive demonstration instead of an unfinished expensive shell

No project guarantees admission. The work should be pursued because it is genuinely worth learning and building.
